Honest version: your browser records about 15 seconds of you talking, and sends it to a model that listens to it. No transcript in the middle.

That matters. Speech-to-text writes standard spelling no matter how you said something — an Egyptian saying gamal and a Levantine saying jamal both come out as جمال. Reading text throws the accent away before anything can judge it.

So it hears the sound itself: which consonants you soften, how your vowels sit, your rhythm. Then it answers with a point on the map and a circle it's honestly confident about, rather than a country and a percentage.

Measured on 45 recordings with known origins: a median miss of 33 km. The old text-based version got the country right 58% of the time.

Your audio is analyzed and discarded. Nothing is stored unless you tick the donate box.
