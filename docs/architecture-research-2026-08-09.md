# Architecture research — 2026-08-09

Commissioned after the serving-drift discovery. Question: the best possible architecture for accent geolocation from short audio, no bias toward the current stack. Six parallel research dossiers + principal-engineer synthesis. Baseline to beat: 37-51 km median (good serving windows) on the 90-clip eval.

# HomeTongue Architecture Decision — Final Recommendation

## Bottom line up front

The current architecture (single audio-native Gemini flash call) is already past published SOTA by roughly 10x (37-51 km vs 481 km academic, vs 1,716 km GPT-4o Audio, vs 3,400-5,600 km open weights on Sonar Moment). Nothing in any dossier beats it on accuracy today. The winnable gains are: (1) killing the 37→69 km serving-window variance with self-consistency ensembling, (2) making the radius honestly per-request instead of globally calibrated, and (3) building the flywheel corpus that is the only credible path below ~30 km. Everything else is a distraction or a future option.

---

## 1. Ranked architectures (top 5)

### #1 — Hardened Gemini committee (recommended)
Pinned dated snapshot + Vertex serving path + k=3-5 parallel flash calls + geometric-median of lat/lng + inter-sample spread driving the 70% radius + hourly golden-clip canary.

- **Expected median:** consistent ~37-48 km. The bad-window ~69 km tail should pull toward good-window numbers because single-sample serving noise gets outvoted; whether the good-window median itself drops a few km is unknown until measured. This is variance reduction, not a median-halving.
- **Cost/analysis:** ~$0.01-0.02 at k=5 (12-20x headroom exists under the $0.05 cap).
- **Monthly fixed:** ~$5-10 (canary calls). No infra.
- **Build effort:** days to 2 weeks solo. Parallel fetches + median math inside the existing Vercel function. Latency unchanged (~11s, parallel).
- **Determinism:** not true determinism, but statistical laundering of drift plus a canary that *detects* bad windows — the practical equivalent for a consumer product.
- **Biggest risk:** rate limits at k=5 under viral load; Vertex path outputs differ from AI Studio, so migration requires one full re-benchmark and radius recalibration.

### #2 — #1 plus disagreement-triggered Pro cascade
When committee spread exceeds a benchmark-tuned threshold, one Gemini 3 Pro call as tiebreaker (Pro is the Sonar Moment leader).

- **Expected median:** same as #1 in the easy mass; improvement concentrated in the worst-quartile clips where error currently blows up. Cascade literature says escalation-on-uncertainty recovers most of the top model's quality. Unproven for this task until the 90-clip run confirms Pro actually beats flash (PolySpeech says flash won dialect ID — do not assume).
- **Cost/analysis:** blended ~$0.015-0.025 with ~20-30% escalation.
- **Monthly fixed:** same as #1.
- **Build effort:** days on top of #1 — the routing signal (spread) is already computed.
- **Determinism:** same as #1.
- **Biggest risk:** Pro may not beat flash at city-level dialects; escalated requests add ~10-15s serial latency (stream the provisional answer).

### #3 — #2 plus retrieval flywheel (many-shot audio ICL) — the 6-month shape
Accent-supervised embedding (fine-tuned XLS-R/ECAPA, SpeechBrain) over donated clips + kNN + top-3-5 neighbour AUDIO clips with ground truth injected into the same Gemini call, region-gated by corpus density.

- **Expected median:** near-term neutral to modestly positive, region-dependent. Long-term the only demonstrated path below ~30 km (Swiss-German text: 21 km median with dense data; PIGEON's classify-then-refine pattern). Also the only defensible moat — nobody can copy the corpus.
- **Cost/analysis:** ~#2 + pennies of input tokens; embedding inference ~free. +1-2s latency.
- **Monthly fixed:** ~$10-50 (vector index, occasional embedding fine-tune runs on rented GPU).
- **Build effort:** weeks; a real second system to maintain.
- **Determinism:** same as #2; the embedding side is fully deterministic.
- **Biggest risk:** attention dilution redux — the same mechanism that made every text-knowledge prompt addition worse. Audio exemplars are a measured different mechanism in the literature, but this ships only if the 90-clip benchmark says it helps, and only in regions with real corpus density (sparse neighbours actively mislead).

### #4 — Current single-call baseline (do nothing)
- **Expected median:** 37-51 km good windows, ~69 km bad — the known quantity.
- **Cost:** <$0.01. **Fixed:** $0. **Effort:** none.
- **Determinism:** none, and no detection of bad windows — this is its real failure, not accuracy.
- **Biggest risk:** silent degradation (Anthropic's own postmortem shows even providers can't detect their drift for weeks) and floating-alias model swaps. Doing nothing still ranks above every alternative stack published — that is how good the baseline is.

### #5 — Self-hosted open weights (Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B / Voxtral Small 24B), later LoRA-fine-tuned on the flywheel
- **Expected median:** est. 1.5-3x worse today (~80-200 km; worst exactly where HomeTongue shines — city-level Arabic). Honest label: unknown until the $30 weekend benchmark. After fine-tuning on 5-10k+ city-labelled clips: plausibly competitive, with zero drift.
- **Cost/analysis:** ~$0.007 at high utilization only.
- **Monthly fixed:** ~$200-700 (one always-warm worker; pure scale-to-zero breaks the 10-15s budget — cold starts run 30-90s without a warm pool).
- **Build effort:** 6-10+ weeks solo, then an ongoing ML program.
- **Determinism:** the only true, bit-exact reproducibility (vLLM batch-invariant mode; ~2x kernel cost irrelevant at low concurrency).
- **Biggest risk:** accuracy regression for months while paying fixed costs; fine-tune brittleness out-of-domain (ADI-20's 33-point collapse on wild audio is the warning). This is a determinism hedge and a fine-tune endgame, not an accuracy play.

## 2. The recommendation

**Now (next 2 weeks):**
1. Pin a dated Gemini snapshot; never a floating alias. Treat every forced migration as a mandatory re-benchmark + radius recalibration event.
2. Ship the self-consistency committee (arch #1): k=3 to start, geometric median, spread → per-request radius. Re-measure the 72% calibration.
3. Stand up the hourly golden-clip canary (5-10 clips). This is the single highest-leverage week of work: it converts "which serving window am I in?" from an unknown into a signal that can gate routing, badge results, or pause a viral push.
4. Run the deciding experiments below (~$40 total).

**1 month:**
5. If the Pro experiment wins: add the disagreement cascade (arch #2).
6. Spend ~$50 training an ECAPA country/region classifier — not for accuracy, but as (a) a sanity check on Gemini outputs, (b) a drift detector independent of Google, and (c) an automatic QA filter for donated clips (reject mislabeled donations before they poison the corpus).
7. Instrument the donation flywheel properly: ground-truth city, "did you grow up there ages 0-12?", mobility flags, mic/channel metadata. The corpus is the strategic asset; label hygiene now determines whether retrieval and fine-tuning work later.

**6 months (each step gated by a corpus milestone, not a date):**
8. At ~2-5k labelled clips with density in specific regions: pilot retrieval as many-shot audio ICL (arch #3), region-gated, benchmark-gated.
9. At ~5-10k clips, or sustained traffic >5-10k analyses/day, or a Google deprecation/degradation event: run the LoRA fine-tune experiment on Qwen3-Omni/Voxtral. If it lands within ~1.5x of Gemini on covered regions, deploy self-hosted as a deterministic second engine ensembled with (not replacing) the frontier call.
10. The sub-30-km endgame is PIGEON-style: Gemini coarse hypothesis + neighbour refinement against the corpus. That is a 2027 conversation that step 7 either enables or forecloses.

## 3. What NOT to do

- **Do not switch API providers for accuracy.** Every published head-to-head has Gemini leading by 7-29 points (dialect ID) or 3-10x (geolocation km). GPT-Audio-mini is 29 points behind; Anthropic has no GA audio API; Nova is preview with zero dialect evidence; xAI's stack is ASR-shaped and strips the signal.
- **Do not build a multi-provider voting committee.** Averaging in members that are 3-10x worse adds noise, not signal. Keep at most one measured non-Gemini path as an outage fallback, never a voting member.
- **Do not LoRA-fine-tune anything now.** The binding constraint is data, not compute: the best trained system on the only city-labelled corpus that exists scores 481 km. All fine-tuning wins in the literature are closed-set classification — a different task. A fine-tune today would regress and lose the open-world lexical knowledge (the "Kuwaiti co-op word" class of evidence) that carries the current accuracy.
- **Do not buy a 24/7 GPU box.** Break-even vs pay-per-call is ~5-10k analyses/day sustained. Pre-viral it is a money pit. Rent by the hour for benchmarks.
- **Do not build a dedicated encoder regressor as the primary model.** That exact architecture IS the 481 km SOTA.
- **Do not use aggregator/reseller endpoints** (silent quantization, unlabeled variants) for a precision-sensitive product.
- **Do not add more written dialect knowledge to the prompt, and do not revisit transcription cascades.** Both already measured worse — the dossiers' independent evidence (PolySpeech, Sonar Moment) confirms latent knowledge beats written guides, and projector/cascade architectures lose the accent signal.

## 4. The deciding experiments (all on the existing 90-clip harness)

1. **k-sweep, ~$5, one day.** k ∈ {1,3,5}, geometric median, run once in a good window and once in a bad window (schedule off-peak vs peak PT). Settles: how much of 37→69 km is variance, and the optimal k. Also yields for free the **spread-vs-error correlation** — if spread predicts error, both the honest radius and the cascade router are validated in one shot.
2. **Gemini 3 Pro, pinned, same prompt, ~$1-2, one hour.** Settles the cascade target. If Pro doesn't beat flash on your deck, drop arch #2's escalation entirely.
3. **Qwen3.5-Omni-Plus via DashScope, ~$0 (free quota), 1-2 days.** The only challenger with a public claim of beating a Gemini Pro tier on audio. Even a tie makes it a measured second-provider fallback for bad windows/outages.
4. **GPT Audio (full), ~$3, one hour.** Expectation low; closes the OpenAI question permanently.
5. **Vertex vs AI Studio A/B on the same snapshot, ~$2.** Settles whether the Vertex migration changes calibration before you commit to it.
6. **Weekend open-weights run, ~$30-50 rented H100.** Qwen3-Omni-30B + Voxtral Small 24B through the deck via vLLM. Replaces the "1.5-3x worse" estimate with a number and establishes a deterministic regression reference for future benchmark deltas.

Total: under $100 and about a week of elapsed time to convert every major unknown in this decision into a measurement. Expectation to hold while running them: the numbers will most likely confirm that the current model is the right engine, that ensembling is the right wrapper, and that the corpus — not the architecture — is what eventually gets HomeTongue below 30 km.

# Research dossiers (raw findings)


## academic-sota

- The ~481 km speech-geolocation SOTA is 'Learning the Arabic Dialect Continuum as a Continuous Space: A Regression Approach to Speaker Origin Prediction' (arXiv 2607.19751, July 2026). Architecture: XLS-R-300M + Whisper-large-v3 encoder features + handcrafted phonotactics, fused by a 4-layer Transformer with attention pooling, predicting a unit-norm 3D point on the sphere with a spherical geodesic loss plus auxiliary country/city softmax heads. Data: only 2,329 ten-second radio clips (ARCADE corpus), 19 countries, 46 cities, leakage-free GroupKFold. — Median error 481.2 km, mean 901.5 km; 13.7% within 100 km; country accuracy 64.5%, city accuracy 45.2%. Best country: Egypt 153.6 km median. Unseen-city zero-shot degrades mean error 1.32x (889 -> 1173 km). Border regions: country accuracy collapses 70.2% -> 33.3%. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19751)
- Closest thing to city-level published: 'Audio-Based Classification and Geographic Regression of Austrian Dialects' (Gutscher & Pucher, Interspeech 2025). Fine-tuned MMS-LID (968M, wav2vec2 family) regressing lat/lng with MSE loss, on 304 hand-curated strong-dialect speakers (elderly, rural, lifelong residents) from 108 Austrian locations. Regression beat location-classification by 30-42 km for every SSL model; ECAPA-TDNN was worst. — Best mean distance error 66.7 km (fold 1) / 65.3 km (10-fold), inside one small country where predicting the geographic center already gives 146 km. XLSR coord: 77.8 km. Dialect-group accuracy 72.7% (6 classes). Location-level accuracy only ~10% over 108 locations. (https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/gutscher25_interspeech.pdf)
- ADI-17 Arabic dialect ID SOTA (~94-97%, country-level 17-way classification, 3,033 h training data): 'Yet Another Model for Arabic Dialect Identification' (arXiv 2310.13812, 2023) reaches 96.9% via ensemble fusion of ECAPA-TDNN + ResNet over MFCC and UniSpeech-SAT-Large features. ADI-20 (Interspeech 2025, arXiv 2511.10070) trains Whisper-large encoder + attention pooling: 95.66% F1 on ADI-17-full, best system 95.82%; ECAPA-TDNN 93.16% vs Whisper-base 91.34% at equal ~20M params. Critically, the best ADI-20 system evaluated zero-shot on the out-of-domain Casablanca TV-drama set collapses to 62.74% F1 (prior SOTA HuBERT-17: 39.24%). — In-domain: 96.9% (ensemble), 95.8% (Whisper-large encoder). Out-of-domain zero-shot: 62.7%. Training needs 53+ h per dialect; 10 h/dialect drops Whisper-large to 92.96%. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10070)
- Accent/language classification SOTA context: VoxLingua107 (107 languages, 6,628 h) is solved to ~93-96% by ECAPA-TDNN (6.7% error; best variants ~4.2%). CommonAccent (Interspeech 2023, arXiv 2305.18283) fine-tunes XLSR/wav2vec2 to ~95% on 16 English accent classes from Common Voice. — VoxLingua107 error 6.7% (ECAPA-TDNN baseline), 4.17% best. CommonAccent English 95% over 16 coarse accent classes (US/UK/India/etc.). (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18283)
- Audio-LLMs vs dedicated models, 2026 evidence: PolySpeech-100 benchmark (arXiv 2606.01016) finds Gemini-3-flash is overall SOTA at 85.30% across 100+ languages/dialects, ahead of every open-source E2E model and GPT-Audio-mini (56.63%); on 19 Chinese regional dialects Gemini-3-flash also leads (83.54%, vs Qwen2.5-Omni 78.61%). The Sonar Moment audio-geolocation benchmark (arXiv 2601.03227, environmental audio not speech) likewise found Gemini 3 Pro strongest (19% of clips within 10 km), with the best open-source audio model averaging 4,853 km error. — Gemini-3-flash 85.3% overall / 83.5% Chinese dialects; GPT-Audio-mini 56.6%; best open audio model on geo-localization: 4,853 km average error vs Gemini leading tier. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01016)
- Text-based geolocation shows what data density buys: transformer models on Swiss-German social-media text reach ~21 km median / 31 km mean within German-speaking Switzerland (tweet-geolocation line of work, e.g. arXiv 2303.07865 and VarDial/linguistic-area work), i.e., sub-25-km medians are achievable when a region has dense labelled data at neighborhood scale. — 21.2 km median, 30.6 km mean, but only within one ~250-km-wide, extremely dialect-dense region with large corpora. (https://arxiv.org/html/2303.07865v4)
- Perceptual-dialectology baseline: large-scale human listener studies (Journal of Linguistic Geography, Cambridge) report native listeners geolocating fellow countrymen's accents with ~66 km average error inside their own country. — ~66 km average human error, single-country setting. (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-linguistic-geography/article/social-factors-in-accent-recognition-a-largescale-study-in-perceptual-dialectology/3E0055501F7EC39C805CF7E1B5640B7A)

**Dossier verdict:** No published approach comes anywhere near HomeTongue's measured 37-51 km median on diverse, worldwide, city-level data — the system is not chasing academic SOTA, it IS past it by roughly an order of magnitude. The actual published landscape: (1) the ~481 km figure is arXiv 2607.19751 (Jul 2026), an Arabic-only coordinate-regression model (XLS-R + Whisper-large-v3 fusion, spherical geodesic loss) trained on just 2,329 ten-second radio clips — 481.2 km median, 45.2% city accuracy, and it degrades 1.32x on unseen cities; (2) the best in-country result is Austrian dialect regression (Interspeech 2025): 65-67 km MEAN inside one small country using a 968M-param fine-tuned MMS model on hand-picked maximally-dialectal speakers with every test location seen in training; (3) ADI-17/ADI-20 Arabic dialect ID reaches 95.8-96.9% but is country-level 17-20-way classification on in-domain broadcast audio, and collapses to 62.7% F1 out-of-domain — a warning about fine-tune brittleness in HomeTongue's wild-microphone setting; (4) 2026 benchmarks (PolySpeech-100, Sonar Moment) independently find Gemini-family audio LLMs beat all open-source audio models and most dedicated pipelines on dialect and audio-geolocation tasks, corroborating HomeTongue's own measurement that Gemini's latent knowledge wins. What the literature does teach: coordinate regression with geodesic loss beats discrete classification (Austrian paper: -30 to -42 km); dedicated fine-tunes need ~50+ h per class and dense location coverage to work; and text-based work shows sub-25-km medians ARE achievable in data-dense regions (Swiss German: 21 km median), so the path below 37 km is the data flywheel, not an architecture swap. Recommended trajectory: keep the Gemini audio-native call as the core; add cheap self-consistency ensembling (median-of-N geometric aggregation) to kill the bad-serving-window tail; use donated ground-truth clips to train an inference-side calibration layer (per-region bias + radius recalibration); and only build a self-hosted fine-tuned SSL regression model once the flywheel has real regional density — at which point its value is determinism and drift-immunity first, accuracy second. Nothing published today justifies abandoning the current stack for accuracy reasons.

## open-weights

Runs in BF16 on a single H100 80GB; Q4 quant fits in ~22GB VRAM (a 4090/L40S becomes viable, tight with audio encoder + KV cache). — 30.5B total / 3.3B active; Apache 2.0; 1x H100 BF16; ~21.9GB at Q4_K_M; SOTA 32/36 audio benchmarks vs models one generation older than your baseline (https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct)

Gemini's frontier-scale latent dialect-to-place knowledge still leads open weights on exactly the sub-skill HomeTongue depends on. — Gemini-3-flash 85.30% vs Qwen2.5-Omni 78.61% on dialect ID (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01016)

At $2.50/hr and ~10s inference, per-analysis compute is ~$0.007 — under your $0.05 cap, but ONLY at high utilization; at low traffic the fixed monthly cost dominates and Gemini's ~<$0.01 pay-per-call wins. The right pattern for spiky viral traffic is serverless GPU (Modal / RunPod Serverless / Baseten) with scale-to-zero or a 1-warm-worker floor; cold-loading ~60GB of weights costs 30-60s unless you use Modal's memory snapshotting, so a warm floor is effectively required to hold your 10-15s latency SLO. Latency itself is fine: a 3B-active MoE on H100 via vLLM decodes a few hundred JSON tokens well inside 10s, and vLLM guided decoding gives you hard schema enforcement Gemini can't match. — H100 $1.50-2.70/hr; A100 $1.39/hr; L40S $0.39+/hr; 4090 $0.34+/hr; 24/7 H100 ~$1.1-2k/mo; ~$0.007/analysis at full utilization (https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/runpod-pricing-vs-thunder-compute)
- What self-hosting actually buys, honestly stated: (1) Fixed weights kill serving drift — your 37-51km-good-window vs 69km-bad-window problem disappears because you pin model+vLLM version forever; caveat: vLLM at temperature=0 is still not bit-deterministic under varying batch composition, but the output DISTRIBUTION is fixed, which is the property you need. (2) Fine-tunability is the real strategic asset: Qwen-Omni models have working audio LoRA paths (ms-swift, LLaMA-Factory), so the donated-clip flywheel can be trained INTO the model — the only architecture where accuracy compounds with your data instead of being frozen at Google's whim. A geolocation-SFT'd Qwen3-Omni on ~5-10k labelled clips is the plausible scenario where open weights eventually beat frozen Gemini flash; nothing beats it out of the box. — Fine-tune trigger point: ~5-10k labelled donated clips; benchmark cost to find out today: ~$30 of on-demand H100 hours against your existing 90-clip harness (https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Omni)

**Dossier verdict:** No open-weights model is plausibly competitive with gemini-3.6-flash at city-level speech geolocation out of the box today. The task's bottleneck is not hearing the accent (Qwen3-Omni, Step-Audio 2, Kimi-Audio all hear paralinguistics well) but the frontier-scale latent knowledge that maps micro-dialect features to specific cities and coordinates — and the best published proxy (PolySpeech-100 dialect ID: Gemini-3-flash 85.3% vs best open 78.6%) plus your own finding that latent knowledge beats written guides both point the same way. Expect the best open model, Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B (Apache 2.0, vLLM, single H100, ~$0.007/analysis at utilization), to land around 1.5-3x your median error uncalibrated, degrading most exactly where HomeTongue shines (Aleppo/Damascus/Amman-grade Arabic distinctions). BUT self-hosting is not therefore worthless — it is the determinism hedge (fixed weights permanently kill the 37→69km serving-drift problem) and, more importantly, the only endgame where the data flywheel compounds: audio LoRA on Qwen3-Omni over ~5-10k donated labelled clips is the realistic scenario where an open model overtakes a frozen Gemini. Concrete recommendation: keep Gemini as the production engine; spend one weekend and ~$30 of on-demand H100 running Qwen3-Omni (and optionally Voxtral Small 24B) through the existing 90-clip harness to replace this estimate with a number; revisit self-hosting seriously when either (a) the flywheel crosses a few thousand labelled clips, or (b) sustained traffic exceeds ~5-10k analyses/day where the fixed GPU cost beats per-call pricing. Do not buy a 24/7 GPU box today.

## finetuning

- Published academic SOTA for speech geolocation (the ~481 km number in the brief) is a TRAINED dedicated-encoder system, not zero-shot: frozen XLS-R-300M + Whisper-large-v3 embeddings + phonotactic features into a 4-layer Transformer with spherical geodesic regression, trained on the ARCADE Arabic radio corpus. It is roughly 10x WORSE than HomeTongue's current zero-shot Gemini baseline (37-51 km median). — 481.2 km pooled median, 901.5 km mean, 64.5% country accuracy, 45.2% city accuracy, 51.7% within 500 km. Training data: only 2,329 samples across 19 countries / 46 cities. Unseen-city zero-shot degrades to 1,173 km mean (1.32x). Best per-country medians: Egypt 153.6 km, Sudan 232.2 km, Algeria 307.1 km. (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19751v1)
- No public corpus provides speaker origin at city level at meaningful scale. ARCADE (2,329 samples, 46 cities, city-level via radio-station geocoding) is the largest city-labelled Arabic set found. Everything bigger is country-level at best. — ARCADE: 2,329 samples / 46 cities / 19 countries; many cities have <50 samples (Qatar, Bahrain, Oman each <50). (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19751v1)
- ADI-17 / ADI-20 are the largest Arabic dialect corpora but are COUNTRY-level only, scraped from YouTube (research-use licensing is murky for a commercial product). Fine-tuned ECAPA-TDNN models get ~90-93% F1 at 17-20-way country classification, and a 53-hour subset nearly matches the full 3,556 hours. — ADI-17: ~3,000 h train + 57 h dev/test, 17 countries. ADI-20: 3,556 h, 19 dialects + MSA. ECAPA-TDNN (20.8M params) trained on ADI-20-53h subset: 92.89% F1 on ADI-17, 90.49% on ADI-20; only a small F1 drop using 30% of training data. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10070)
- Mozilla Common Voice's accent metadata is self-reported free text, overwhelmingly for ENGLISH, and mostly country/region-level (grew from 164 to 235 distinct accent labels). Common Voice Arabic is Modern Standard Arabic read speech - useless for dialect geolocation, which is the flagship use case. A 2026 survey of Arabic speech resources confirms almost no datasets exist below country-level geographic granularity. — 235 accent labels in recent CV versions (vs 164 initially); Arabic subset is MSA only. CommonAccent (fine-tuned ECAPA/wav2vec2 on CV English) reaches ~95% accuracy on a 16-accent closed set. (https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_12_0)
- Other candidate corpora are small, coarse, or licence-blocked for a consumer product: Speech Accent Archive is ~2,959 speakers each reading ONE English paragraph, with city-level birthplace, but CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial - cannot legally power hometongue.me). VoxPopuli's accented subset is 29 h of L2 English across 15 accents at country level (EU politicians). IARPA Babel is LDC-licensed telephone speech, country-level, fee-based. Wikitongues is oral-history video, tiny per language, mixed licences. — Speech Accent Archive: 2,959 samples (as of 2021), ~20 s read English each, CC BY-NC-SA 2.0. VoxPopuli: 400K h unlabelled / 1,791 h transcribed / 4,295 speakers, 29 h accented-English subset, 15 L2 accents, country-level. (https://accent.gmu.edu/about.php)
- On closed-set dialect tasks, fine-tuned small models crush zero-shot frontier LLMs - but the gap runs the OTHER way for HomeTongue's open-world task. Example: Indian-language dialect classification, zero-shot GPT-4o scored F1 4.25 and Gemini 2.5 Pro 19.62 while every fine-tuned model dramatically outperformed them; Chinese regional dialect ID: Gemini-3-flash 83.5% vs Qwen2.5-Omni 78.6% zero-shot. Yet HomeTongue's zero-shot Gemini beats the trained geolocation SOTA 10x because the LLM brings lexical world knowledge (e.g. recognizing a Kuwaiti co-op word) that no acoustic encoder trained on 2-3k samples can have. — GPT-4o zero-shot F1 4.25 vs fine-tuned wav2vec2-based models on Indic dialects; Gemini-3-flash 83.54% / Qwen2.5-Omni 78.61% on Chinese dialect ID; contrast: trained SOTA 481 km vs HomeTongue zero-shot 37-51 km. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10388)
- Open-weight audio LLMs suitable for LoRA fine-tuning exist under Apache 2.0: Mistral Voxtral Mini 3B / Small 24B (speech understanding, built on Ministral 3B / Mistral Small 3.1) and the Qwen-Omni family (Qwen2.5/Qwen3-Omni; Qwen3-ASR covers 52 languages/dialects). Community fine-tuning tooling (LoRA on the audio adapter + LM) is established. — Voxtral Mini 3B and Small 24B, Apache 2.0; Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602 open weights, 8.87 GB. LoRA/QLoRA cost benchmarks 2026: 7-8B model $3-10 per run (2-4 h rented A100, or <$3 on an RTX 4090); QLoRA on H100 8-12 h = $10-16; a full experimentation campaign (dozens of runs + evals) realistically $100-500 total. An ECAPA-class encoder head (20.8M params) on frozen embeddings trains on one consumer GPU in hours, effectively <$50. (https://www.stratagem-systems.com/blog/lora-fine-tuning-cost-analysis-2026)
- How much data city-level needs, extrapolating from the literature: ARCADE's masking experiments show held-out-city performance is determined by coverage of the geographic/dialect neighborhood, and ADI-20 shows per-class saturation at roughly 2-3 h/class for country ID. City-level worldwide implies hundreds-to-thousands of classes needing on the order of 50-200 speakers (or ~1-3 h) per city of spontaneous speech - i.e., order 10k-100k+ labelled clips before a fine-tune could plausibly compete with the current 37-51 km zero-shot baseline. HomeTongue's donation flywheel is the only realistic source. — ADI-20: 53 h / 20 classes (~2.6 h per class) retains ~93% F1. ARCADE: 46 cities x ~50 samples median already gives 45.2% city accuracy on SEEN cities but 1,173 km mean on unseen. Target scale estimate: 10^4-10^5 city-labelled clips. (https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10070)

**Dossier verdict:** Fine-tuning is NOT the path to beat the current architecture today, and the reason is data, not compute or skill. The best published trained system for this exact task (frozen XLS-R+Whisper embeddings -> geodesic regression, trained on the only city-labelled Arabic corpus that exists: 2,329 samples / 46 cities) achieves 481 km median error - roughly 10x worse than HomeTongue's zero-shot Gemini baseline of 37-51 km. Every corpus a solo dev can legally use commercially is country-level at best (ADI-17/20: 3,000-3,556 h but 17-20 country classes and YouTube-scraped licensing; Common Voice: CC0 but MSA-only Arabic and free-text mostly-country accents; VoxPopuli: 29 h country-level L2 English; Speech Accent Archive: city-level birthplaces but ~3,000 read-speech clips under a non-commercial licence). The literature's fine-tuning wins (95% CommonAccent, 93% ADI, GPT-4o F1 4.25 vs fine-tuned models on Indic dialects) are all CLOSED-SET classification - a different task from open-world city geolocation, where the frontier LLM's lexical world knowledge (the Kuwaiti co-op word) is exactly what a 2-3k-sample fine-tune can never acquire. Concrete recommendation: (1) do NOT LoRA-fine-tune an audio LLM now; (2) DO spend ~$50 training an ECAPA/WavLM country-region classifier (SpeechBrain recipe, ADI-20-53h-scale data, ~90-93% F1) as an ensemble prior, Gemini-drift detector, and donated-clip QA filter - it directly attacks the serving-reproducibility problem at near-zero cost; (3) treat the data flywheel as the strategic asset: at roughly 10k-100k donated clips with city ground truth (the scale ARCADE's coverage analysis implies), revisit LoRA on Voxtral Small 24B / Qwen3-Omni ($100-500 training, one GPU box serving) as a deterministic, drift-immune self-hosted engine - likely deployed as a hybrid that ensembles with, rather than replaces, the frontier call. Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19751v1 (481 km SOTA + ARCADE), https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10070 (ADI-20), https://github.com/swshon/arabic-dialect-identification (ADI-17), https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18283 (CommonAccent), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10388 (zero-shot LLM vs fine-tuned on dialects), https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.80/ (VoxPopuli), https://accent.gmu.edu/about.php (Speech Accent Archive), https://huggingface.co/datasets/mozilla-foundation/common_voice_12_0 (Common Voice), https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2/ (Voxtral licensing), https://www.stratagem-systems.com/blog/lora-fine-tuning-cost-analysis-2026 (LoRA costs).

## retrieval-ensemble

- The Sonar Moment benchmark (AGL1K, Jan 2026) is the first systematic audio-LLM geolocation benchmark. Gemini 3 Pro is far ahead of every alternative provider: any multi-provider committee would average in members 3-10x worse than Gemini. It also found reasoning/'thinking' modes give no consistent gain, and the top failure modes are over-commitment to a single clue and language ambiguity - exactly what sample-agreement ensembling counteracts. — Median error: Gemini 3 Pro 480 km, Gemini 2.5 Pro 548 km, GPT-4o Audio 1,716 km, Mimo-Audio 3,451 km, Qwen3-Omni 5,627 km. Speech presence drops Gemini error 3,727 km -> 1,548 km (linguistic content dominates environmental sound). (https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03227)
- The published academic SOTA HomeTongue already beats 10x is GeoArc-FF (Jul 2026): frozen XLS-R-300M + Whisper-large-v3 features, Transformer backbone, spherical geodesic regression, trained on the ARCADE Arabic radio corpus (2,329 clips, 19 countries, 46 cities). Its key failure: unseen cities degrade 1.32x and isolated cities 'fail severely' - accuracy is corpus-density-bound. — Median 481.2 km, mean 901.5 km, country accuracy 64.5%, city accuracy 45.2%, within-500km 51.7%, trained on ~1,860 clips/fold. Earlier NAACL 2024 radio-broadcast geolocation models: ~650 km. HomeTongue baseline: 37-69 km median. (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19751v1)
- Speech embeddings DO cluster geographically, but only weakly off-the-shelf: a 60-language Indic study (Whisper-base, Wav2Vec2) found fine-grained geographic subclusters form when the encoder is fine-tuned with joint language+district supervision, not raw SSL embeddings. CommonAccent showed ECAPA-TDNN/XLSR reach ~95% English accent classification, so accent identity is recoverable from these encoders once supervised. — District F1 91.29 with joint language+district supervision vs 80.89 language-only (Whisper-base); CommonAccent 95% accent-ID accuracy across EN/IT/DE/ES Common Voice. (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19940)
- PIGEON (CVPR 2024) is the proven template for the retrieval hybrid in the vision analog of this exact problem: semantic geocell classification + retrieval-over-location-clusters refinement, where final position is picked by embedding distance to known reference points within top-K cells. This classify-coarse-then-refine-by-neighbour pattern is what converts region-level to street/city-level accuracy - but it works because Street View gives dense reference coverage everywhere. — Median error 44.4 km globally; >40% of guesses within 25 km; >5% within 1 km. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05845)
- Self-consistency (sample k outputs, aggregate) is the best-evidenced ensemble technique for a single strong model: +11 to +18 pp on reasoning benchmarks via majority vote, extended to multi-model weighted voting. For continuous lat/lng the aggregate is the geometric median of k sampled points, and the spread of the k points is a direct, measurable uncertainty signal - which is exactly what an honest 70%-radius needs and what serving-drift variance corrupts. — +17.9% GSM8K, +11.0% SVAMP, +12.2% AQuA from majority voting over sampled paths; adaptive variants cut sampling cost up to 7.9x. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13855)
- LLM cascade literature (FrugalGPT, UCCI, RouteLLM) shows confidence-thresholded escalation from cheap to expensive models preserves ~95-98% of top-model quality at a fraction of cost; calibrated-uncertainty routing (isotonic regression on a confidence score) is the standard trigger. For HomeTongue the economics invert: flash is already <$0.01, so the cascade is used to BUY accuracy (escalate to Gemini Pro, the AGL1K leader) on the minority of clips where the flash committee disagrees, not to save money. — FrugalGPT: matches GPT-4 with up to 98% cost reduction or +4% accuracy at equal cost; RouteLLM: 95% of GPT-4 quality at 15% of cost. Escalating ~20-30% of traffic to a Pro-tier call keeps blended cost well under $0.05. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18796)
- Many-shot multimodal in-context learning is the low-lift way to use retrieval WITHOUT changing providers: audio exemplars with labels in-context improve speech tasks consistently ('In-Context Learning Boosts Speech Recognition via Human-like Adaptation to Speakers and Language Varieties'; Many-Shot ICL shows log-linear gains up to ~2,000 multimodal examples, approaching fine-tuning). Retrieved donated clips (audio + ground-truth city) can be fed to the same Gemini call as few-shot evidence. — Many-shot multimodal ICL: substantial gains over <100-shot across datasets, log-linear scaling, near fine-tuned performance; audio-transcript in-context examples improved ASR on all tested corpora. (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.11018)

**Dossier verdict:** Keep audio-native Gemini as the core - nothing in the 2026 literature comes within an order of magnitude of it on speech geolocation (AGL1K: Gemini 480 km vs GPT-4o 1,716 km vs open models 3,400-5,600 km; academic specialist SOTA 481 km vs HomeTongue's measured 37-69 km). The best architecture beyond a single call is a three-stage evolution, in strict ROI order: (1) NOW - self-consistency: 3-5 parallel flash samples, geometric-median aggregation, inter-sample spread driving a per-request honest radius; this converts the model's non-determinism and serving drift from a liability into a measurable uncertainty signal, at ~$0.02/analysis and unchanged latency. (2) NEXT - cascade on disagreement: when the committee's spread crosses a benchmark-tuned threshold, escalate that clip to Gemini 3 Pro; the routing signal is free and the cost stays consumer-viable. (3) FLYWHEEL - retrieval as many-shot audio ICL: fine-tune an accent-supervised embedding (XLS-R/ECAPA, SpeechBrain) on donated clips, kNN, and inject neighbour AUDIO with ground truth into the same Gemini call - but region-gated by corpus density and gated by the 90-clip benchmark, because raw SSL embeddings cluster geography only weakly and sparse neighbours mislead (the attention-dilution finding will repeat if this ships unmeasured). Reject multi-provider committees (weaker members are noise) and defer any self-trained regressor until the corpus reaches tens of thousands of labelled clips, at which point a PIGEON-style geocell-classify-plus-neighbour-refine specialist becomes the plausible sub-30-km endgame and the corpus itself is the moat.

## serving-reality

- Anthropic's September 2025 engineering postmortem documents three overlapping infrastructure bugs that silently degraded Claude output quality for weeks (context-window routing error, TPU server misconfiguration corrupting token generation, and an XLA:TPU approximate top-k miscompilation). Detection took roughly a month even inside Anthropic. The postmortem also states explicitly that Anthropic never intentionally degrades quality with load. — At the worst hour (Aug 31, 2025) 16% of Sonnet 4 requests were affected; Haiku 3.5 was affected for almost two weeks; degradation ran early August to early September before the fix. (https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/a-postmortem-of-three-recent-issues)
- Google's own AI developer forum carries multiple threads on Gemini Flash quality degradation across generations (2.0, 2.5, 3.6), including one specifically on gemini-3.6-flash, and community-collected data showing failure rates strongly correlated with time-of-day load. Google does not acknowledge load-dependent quality (as opposed to availability) anywhere in its docs. — Community data (Dec 2025-Feb 2026): peak-hour failure rates ~45% dominated by 503 'model is overloaded'; off-peak (2-7 AM PT) below 5%. (https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/feedback-significant-quality-degradation-in-gemini-3-6-flash-model-code-modification-failures/176502)
- No major API provider offers real determinism. OpenAI's seed + system_fingerprint is documented as 'mostly deterministic' with variance even when fingerprint matches, and the fingerprint changes a few times a year. Gemini's seed is documented as 'best effort... deterministic output isn't guaranteed', with an open GitHub issue reproducing determinism failure at fixed seed and temperature. Anthropic offers no seed parameter at all. HomeTongue already measured that gemini-3.6-flash ignores temperature entirely. — OpenAI: system_fingerprint infra changes 'a few times a year'; longer max_tokens outputs are documented as less deterministic. Gemini issue #745 reliably reproduces different outputs for identical seeded requests on 2.5-pro. (https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/reproducible_outputs_with_the_seed_parameter)
- Silent model swaps behind stable endpoint names are a documented incident class: OpenAI's April 2025 GPT-4o sycophancy update rolled out and was fully rolled back within days, meaning apps calling the same 'gpt-4o' alias hit meaningfully different models with no API-visible signal. OpenAI's mitigation was pushing system-prompt changes server-side overnight — itself a silent behavior change. Dated snapshots (e.g. gpt-4o-2024-08-06 style) are the only alias-stability mechanism OpenAI and Google offer, and Google's are subject to scheduled deprecations that force migrations. — Sycophancy update: rolled out Apr 24-25, 2025; full rollback Apr 28 — 4 days of a different model behind the same name. (https://openai.com/index/sycophancy-in-gpt-4o/)
- Google's only real lever against load-dependent serving is Vertex AI Provisioned Throughput: reserved, dedicated throughput at fixed price (per-GSU), enforced over a dynamic window, on the enterprise Vertex path (SLA-backed) rather than the AI Studio key. Community reports also document output differences for the same Gemini model between AI Studio and Vertex serving paths, so a migration requires re-benchmarking. OpenRouter-style aggregators openly list per-endpoint quantization because third-party hosts do serve quantized variants — unlabeled aggregator endpoints are unsuitable for a precision-sensitive task. — Provisioned Throughput is fixed-price per GSU with a purchase commitment; Vertex tiles images at ~1800 tokens differently from AI Studio (vision path evidence that serving paths differ). (https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/provisioned-throughput/use-provisioned-throughput)
- Self-hosted fixed-weights inference is NOT deterministic by default: vLLM's own reproducibility docs state that identical seeded greedy requests produce different outputs run-to-run because concurrent traffic changes batch composition and Torch kernels are not batch-invariant, and divergence compounds token by token. This is now solvable: Thinking Machines' 'Defeating Nondeterminism in LLM Inference' (Sept 2025) released batch-invariant kernels, and vLLM has since shipped a first-class batch-invariance mode that makes output independent of batch size and request order, at a performance cost (unoptimized: roughly 2x slower on Qwen3-8B; optimized attention kernels reduce it). — ~2x single-GPU slowdown unoptimized for batch-invariant mode; online serving can only get determinism via batch invariance (seed alone is insufficient). (https://docs.vllm.ai/en/latest/features/batch_invariance/)
- Serverless GPU cold starts are incompatible with a 10-15s end-to-end budget unless aggressively engineered: unoptimized vLLM cold starts run ~50s (measured baseline) up to 460s with large models; RunPod FlashBoot best case is ~0.5-0.6s but only when the snapshot captured a loaded model, with measured worst cases of 42s and 30-90s without a warm pool; Modal's GPU snapshots restore vLLM in 2.25s (from S3) to 9s (local NVMe) vs ~50s full boot. Practical 99% uptime for a solo dev is a warm-pool of 1-2 workers or one always-on GPU, not multi-region (99% allows ~7h/month downtime; single-region + API fallback covers it). — Cold starts: 0.56s best / 42s max (RunPod measured); 30-90s no warm pool; Modal snapshot restore 2.25-9s vs ~50s; warm H100 endpoints 8-15s to first token. (https://modal.com/blog/truly-serverless-gpus)
- There is exactly one credible open-weights audio-native family a solo dev can serve on vLLM today for this task: Mistral's Voxtral (Voxtral-Mini-3B and Voxtral-Small-24B, Apache-style open weights on Hugging Face, first-class vLLM support, audio-in with 30-40 min context; 24B runs on a single 48GB-class GPU, 3B on 16GB), plus Qwen's Omni line via vllm-omni. No other frontier-quality audio-understanding model has self-hostable weights. — Voxtral: 3B and 24B params; 32k token context; 30-40 min audio; 24B fits ~48GB VRAM (less quantized). (https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507)

**Dossier verdict:** The August 2026 serving-reality verdict: quality drift behind stable API endpoints is a documented industry-wide phenomenon, not a Gemini quirk — Anthropic's own postmortem shows weeks-long silent degradation (16% of Sonnet 4 requests at peak) that even the provider couldn't detect quickly, OpenAI shipped and rolled back a materially different GPT-4o behind the same alias in 4 days, and Google's forums corroborate load-correlated Gemini Flash failure rates (~45% peak vs <5% off-peak). No API offers real determinism (OpenAI: 'mostly'; Gemini: 'best effort' with reproduced failures; Anthropic: no seed at all), and even self-hosted vLLM is nondeterministic by default — only vLLM's new batch-invariance mode (from Thinking Machines' Sept 2025 work) delivers true bit-identical inference, at a ~2x kernel cost that is irrelevant at HomeTongue's concurrency. Given that the accuracy moat (37-51 km vs ~481 km academic SOTA) comes from a frontier model's latent dialect knowledge that no open-weights audio model matches, the best architecture is NOT to abandon the API: keep a pinned dated Gemini snapshot as the accuracy engine, move it to the Vertex serving path, wrap it in k=3 self-consistency aggregation to statistically launder serving noise, and run an hourly golden-clip canary so bad windows are detected, routed around, or surfaced honestly. Treat every snapshot migration as a mandatory re-calibration event. Self-hosting Voxtral-class open weights is today a reproducibility instrument and a fine-tuning bet, not an accuracy play — it becomes the endgame only once the donated-clip flywheel yields a corpus (roughly 5-10k labelled clips) large enough to fine-tune city-level knowledge into weights the product controls forever. Serverless GPU numbers (RunPod 0.56s best/42s worst, 30-90s without warm pool; Modal snapshot restores 2.25-9s vs ~50s full vLLM boot) mean any self-host path must budget for one always-warm worker (~$200-700/mo), and 99% uptime for a solo dev is one region plus an API fallback, not multi-region.
