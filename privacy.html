Privacy, in plain words

HomeTongue listens to your accent and guesses where you grew up, in any language. Here's exactly what happens to your voice.

By default: your voice and words are never kept

When you talk, your recording is sent to Google's Gemini model, held in memory long enough to answer one question about it, and discarded. It is never written to disk. There is no transcription step and no separate speech-to-text service — the model listens to the audio directly. No account, no name, no email, no tracking pixels. If you answer "did I get it?", we keep an anonymous scorecard only: the country code we guessed, the broad region, the confidence score, whether you said we were right, the country you corrected us to, and a coarse device type (phone or desktop). Never your recording, never your words, never a city.

If you tick the "donate this clip" box

We additionally keep that one recording, its transcript, your correction including the city if you typed one, and the guess details above, with a timestamp. We use it for one purpose: training a model that recognizes accents better, especially at the city level where no data exists anywhere in the world. Donated clips may become part of a research dataset; they are never sold, never used for ads, and never linked to your identity — we don't know who you are.

Deleting a donated clip

Since there are no accounts, tell us roughly when you donated (date, time, and the guess the app made) and we'll find and delete it: email mohd.e.arab@gmail.com, or open an issue here.

Third parties we use

Accent analysis runs on Google (Gemini). Hosting is Vercel, and donated clips live in Supabase storage. Each processes your audio only to provide the service. HomeTongue used to send audio to Groq (Whisper) for transcription and Anthropic (Claude) for analysis; that pipeline was replaced in August 2026 and neither receives your audio any more.

Guess the Voice, the listening game

The game is separate from your voice and never records you. If you post a score, we store the nickname you type, the score, and which clip and distance each round was — that is the leaderboard, and it is public. Pick any nickname you like; we have no way of knowing who you are, and there is no account to delete.

Most decks stream their clips from YouTube so the original creator keeps the view and the credit. Those are real YouTube embeds, which means YouTube can set its own cookies and see that a video was played, exactly as if you had opened it on youtube.com. That is the one third party on this site that can see anything about your visit, and it only appears on the game page — never on the page where you record your voice.
